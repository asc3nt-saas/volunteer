      Can't find your thing? Start it.

      The best student projects began with one person noticing a gap. You don't need a big team or a budget to begin.

      1. Spot the needAsk a teacher, a neighbour or a local charity what they're short of.
      2. Find two friendsThree people is enough to run a first session and cover for each other.
      3. Get permissionCheck with your school or university student office. They often have rooms, insurance and small funds.
      4. Run one small testDo a single afternoon first. Ask people what worked, then repeat.

      What you get back

      Real skills
      Teamwork, planning and speaking up. Things exams don't test but employers ask about.
      New people
      You'll meet people from other courses, ages and neighbourhoods.
      A clearer direction
      Trying a field for a few hours is a cheap way to learn if you want to study or work in it.
      A break from screens
      Helping out is a good reset when study stress builds up.

      Before your first time

      • Start small. One hour a week beats a big plan you drop in week three.
      • Ask what the organisation actually needs, instead of what you'd like to do.
      • If you're under 18, check the age rules and tell a parent or teacher where you'll be.
      • Ask for a short reference letter or certificate. It helps with applications later.
      • Say no when you're overloaded. Looking after yourself keeps you able to help.

      Questions students ask

      I'm busy with exams. Is it still worth it?

      Yes, if you keep it small. One hour a week, or one Saturday a term, is enough to count. Pause during exam weeks and tell the organisation in advance.

      Do I need experience?

      No. Most organisations train you on the first day. Your willingness to turn up on time matters more than any skill.

      Can I do it from home?

      Yes. Online tutoring, translating and design work can all be done remotely. Use the "Online & skills" option in the menu.

      Will it help my CV or university application?

      It can. Write down what you did, how many hours, and what changed because of it. Ask the organiser for a short letter or certificate.

      Is it safe?

      Choose registered organisations or ones your school knows, never meet strangers alone, and tell someone where you are. Ask about safety rules before you start.

      Spare Hour is a starting point. Check local rules and ask an adult or your student office if you're unsure about safety.
